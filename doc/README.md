# The mathematics of mps

One note per algorithm family. Each states its result with every hypothesis, proves it or sketches the proof and names where the full proof is published, says what `mps` uses or will use and where, and lists its sources and its provenance.

- [Mersenne numbers, and why the exponent must be prime](mersenne-numbers.md): $a \mid p$ implies $2^a - 1 \mid 2^p - 1$, so a Mersenne prime $M_p$ needs a prime $p$, and the exponent check `mps` runs before any test.
- [The Lucas–Lehmer test](lucas-lehmer.md): $M_p$ for an odd prime $p$ is prime exactly when it divides $s_{p-2}$, with $s_0 = 4$ and $s_{i+1} = s_i^2 - 2$; the Jacobi invariant a run checks, derived here, and what a shifted run computes.
- [Fermat probable primes, and the Gerbicz check](prp-and-gerbicz.md): the base-3 Fermat test of $M_p$ as $p$ squarings from 3, why base 2 says nothing, the Gerbicz check and what it can and cannot detect, and proofs of a PRP result.
- [The reference arithmetic](reference-arithmetic.md): `BigInt`, the integer of 64-bit limbs every engine is checked against; folding modulo $M_p$ and why it ends fully reduced, Res64, the canonical hexadecimal and decimal strings, the schoolbook and Karatsuba products and squares, long division, modular powers and decimal conversion; and the reference Lucas–Lehmer and PRP tests built on it.
- [The form of factors, and trial factoring](factors-and-trial-factoring.md): every prime factor of $M_p$, for an odd prime $p$, is $2kp + 1$ and $\pm 1 \bmod 8$, proved in full, and trial factoring built on it: candidates in classes, the sieve, the powering ladder, its cost, and Montgomery's and Barrett's reductions.
- [Pollard's P−1 method](p-1.md): a prime $q$ divides $\gcd(a^E - 1, N)$ when $q - 1$ divides $E$; why putting $2p$ into $E$ makes the method strong on $M_p$; stages 1 and 2, the faster stage 2s, and a worked example on $M_{67}$.
- [Weighted transforms](weighted-transforms.md): squaring modulo $M_p$ by the irrational-base discrete weighted transform, a cyclic convolution of length $N$ with no zero-padding, its balanced digits and weights, proved in full; Percival's proven error bounds against the conjectured and heuristic ones used to size transforms in practice.
- [Number-theoretic transforms](number-theoretic-transforms.md): exact convolution in a finite field, which fields hold the roots of unity and the $N$-th root of 2 that the weights need, the fields over the Mersenne primes $M_{31}$ and $M_{61}$ and their pairing by the Chinese remainder theorem, and Harvey's lazy reduction.

The bibliography is [references.md](references.md): every work a note or a comment in `src/` cites, each entry checked against the work, with a record of what it was checked against and when.

Everything here follows the clean-room rule of [AGENTS.md, § Invariants 4](../AGENTS.md#4-clean-room-human-gate).

Each concrete number a note states, such as a worked example, a factor, a residue, a count or a published value, sits between two HTML comment markers. Definitions, format constants and theorem values, the general results a proof establishes, are not concrete numbers in this sense and take no markers; a number stated about a specific instance does, and so does a restatement of a number the note marks elsewhere. A check marker carries the computation that verified the number, and a cited marker surrounds a number taken from a published work, beside its located citation. A check is a small integer expression over functions defined in `spec/math-notes-and-references/verify/claims.py`, and `notes.py claims` in that directory evaluates the checks of the notes it is given.
